Optical visibility and a radar decrease are different requirements. A narrow bright surface or cleared verge can alter optical reflectance while occupying only part of a 20 m analysis pixel. Radoux’s separability framework and Oehmcke’s road models support width/contrast-dependent visibility, but do not validate this NDVI/SAR gate [L01, L02]. Apparent sampled widths are only visual estimates, and archived optical source windows are already resampled from nominal 10 m bands to 20 m. No universal minimum detectable width is claimed.

ESA explains how moisture, surface roughness, polarization and incidence influence radar returns [L05]. Maintained/increased backscatter after clearing is therefore a plausible alternative to a VV/VH drop. The actual local moisture state was not measured. The radar summer median and one June optical image describe different temporal summaries. Scene-level RTC compatibility and common orbit do not establish exact local co-registration; the issue report L07 is a caution from another location, not a diagnosed defect here.

CCDC explicitly treats geometric registration and atmosphere as prerequisites, combines temporal screening with repeated observations, and documents loss of useful clear pixels when selecting only clear scenes [L04, Methods]. This supports checking clear-scene sampling bias; it does not calibrate our eight-date reference. BFAST separates trend and seasonality using a longer series [L03, Methods].
